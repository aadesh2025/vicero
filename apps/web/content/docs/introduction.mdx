Vicero lets you build a chatbot that actually knows your business. You give an agent a
persona and a model, point it at your documents, and put it on your website or in the
messaging apps your customers already use. When it cannot help, it hands the conversation
to a person.

The goal is a live, knowledge-grounded chatbot embedded on your site in about fifteen
minutes, using free-tier models, without writing code.

## What you get

**Agents.** A chatbot is an *agent*: a persona, a system prompt, a model and its settings,
and a set of switches for what it is allowed to do. Agents are versioned — you edit a
draft, test it, and publish when it is right. If a published version misbehaves, roll back.

**Knowledge and citations.** Upload PDFs, Word documents, spreadsheets, Markdown or plain
text, or point at a URL. Vicero extracts, splits and embeds the content, then retrieves
the relevant passages at answer time and cites them. An agent grounded this way says "I
don't know" instead of inventing something.

**Channels.** One `<script>` tag puts the chat widget on your site. The same agent can also
answer on Telegram, WhatsApp, Slack, Discord, Instagram and Facebook Messenger, with every
conversation landing in one inbox.

**Human handoff.** Your team works from a shared inbox. They can take over a conversation
mid-thread, reply as themselves, and hand it back to the agent. Conversations carry notes,
tags and the contact's history.

**Automations.** Agents can call tools: look something up, hit an HTTP endpoint, or trigger
an [n8n](https://n8n.io) workflow. Results come back into the conversation.

**Everything measured.** Conversations, resolution rate, handoff rate, latency, tokens and
cost per agent and per provider, the questions that got asked most, and the ones nobody
could answer.

## How the pieces fit

<Callout type="note" title="The shape of a turn">
A visitor sends a message. Vicero finds the relevant passages in your knowledge base,
assembles them with your system prompt and the recent conversation, and streams the model's
answer back token by token — calling tools along the way if the agent is allowed to. The
turn is recorded with its citations, token usage and cost.
</Callout>

An **organization** is your workspace. It holds your agents, knowledge bases, channels,
contacts and team. Everything is scoped to it: data from one organization is never visible
from another.

Inside an organization, people have **roles** — owner, admin, editor, viewer or operator —
that decide who can change an agent, who can publish it, and who can only work the inbox.

## What this documentation assumes

It assumes you have a Vicero account and can sign in. It does not assume you write
Python or TypeScript: everything in the guides can be done from the dashboard, and the API
sections are there for when you want to automate it.
